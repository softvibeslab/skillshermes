# Trend-informed pilot copy

## Evidence ladder

- A platform's annual trend report is a forecast and commercial perspective, not proof of what leads the local feed this week.
- Recent public posts with dates and observable metrics can show examples of performance, not causal proof or private conversions.
- A Top-10 written for a new offer is an editorial shortlist until tested. Avoid calling it a measured ranking.
- Read the actual retrieved content. A successful scraping run or dataset metadata alone is not evidence. Fetch projected URL/text fields and follow secondary citations to primary sources.
- Record source URL and the period it describes. Recheck when reusing; do not preserve trend claims as timeless facts.

## Research example, not an evergreen trend prescription

TikTok Next 2026, https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/next, described real people/processes (Reali-Tea), curiosity-driven discovery, and Emotional ROI. For an SME pilot, the useful interpretation was founder-led process footage, recognizable daily pressures, and explicit practical value. This does not establish Mexican SME preferences or justify copying unrelated trending hashtags.

## Messaging structure

1. Campaign: memorable invitation in the audience's language.
2. Human benefit: relief from a recognizable daily burden.
3. Pilot offer: one workflow, not an entire promised ecosystem.
4. CTA: a conversation or voluntary pilot application the team can actually handle.

Illustrative, unvalidated Softvibes copy:

- Campaign: “Enchula tu PYME con Softvibes.”
- Benefit: “Tu negocio no tiene que vivir en tu cabeza.”
- Offer: “Piloto de organización de pendientes y seguimiento a clientes con IA.”
- CTA: “Cuéntanos qué tarea te está quitando tiempo.”

“Enchula” needs the offer underneath to avoid sounding like a cosmetic brand redesign. Prefer concrete verbs over vague claims about leveraging ecosystems.

## Candidate package

For each candidate provide: hook → short body → recommended format → single CTA. Mix angles rather than ten synonyms:

- Recognition/humor: “Tu CRM se llama ‘ahorita le contesto’.” Laugh with the owner, not at them.
- Emotional burden: “Cierras el negocio. ¿Pero el negocio te deja cerrar el día?”
- Process evidence: founders explain what they are testing, show limitations and invite feedback.
- Educational transition: from asking AI for ideas to recording and following commitments.

These are creative examples, not user-approved brand decisions or proven winners.

## Test and claim discipline

Use a consistent offer and CTA across the initial shortlist. Compare qualified owner conversations and willingness to participate, alongside attention metrics. Different organic audiences and times confound comparisons; do not declare significance from a handful of posts.

Promise only what exists. A desired earlier finishing time is a hypothesis, not a guaranteed saving. A draft is not an automatically sent message. A documented project is not a verified production integration. Never publish, message prospects, or deploy a dashboard merely because copy has been requested.
